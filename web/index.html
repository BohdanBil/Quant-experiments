How it works

Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall

VaR 99% is the loss that is exceeded only 1 time in 100. Expected Shortfall 97.5% answers the follow-up question: when things are worse than that line, how bad on average? It looks at the worst 2.5% of outcomes, which is why banking regulation (Basel FRTB) prefers it.

Monte Carlo simulation

Instead of one formula, the model generates 100,000 possible futures and reads the risk off them. That handles things formulas cannot: holding stocks without rebalancing, options with kinked payoffs, and the worst drop along the way. The cost is noise, which is why the numbers carry ± standard errors and the convergence chart shows how many paths are needed.

Filtered historical simulation with HAR volatility (the main model)

Each historical day is converted into a "shock": the day's returns divided by how volatile the market was expected to be. A simulated day picks a random historical day and replays its shocks at today's volatility, which keeps real fat tails and the real way the stocks moved together.

Volatility comes from a HAR model. It measures each day's volatility from its open, high, low and close, not just the close, and forecasts tomorrow's from the past day, week and month, plus recent falls. Having three time scales lets it handle both short spikes and slower changes in the market's mood.

Backtesting

The models are re-fitted every day using only past data, and their 1-day VaR is compared with what actually happened. The Kupiec test checks the breach rate; the Christoffersen test checks that breaches don't cluster; the Basel traffic light counts breaches per 250 days (0–4 green, 5–9 yellow, 10+ red); the ES ratio compares actual and forecast losses on breach days.

Data and its limits

Dividend-adjusted daily prices from Yahoo Finance since 2000. Holiday rows and bad opening prices are cleaned. Expected returns are set to zero, because two years of data can't estimate them and an optimistic drift would shrink the risk. There are no transaction costs or currency effects, and the 100-day numbers come from a daily model (only the 1-day forecasts are backtested). This is a study, not investment advice.
